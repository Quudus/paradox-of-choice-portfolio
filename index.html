Research Design

Research question: Does the number of product options offered to a shopper affect how long they take to decide, whether they buy, and how satisfied, confident, and regret-free they feel afterward?

A one-factor, two-level between-subjects experiment compared a curated assortment of 4 options versus a larger assortment of 16 options. Participants were randomly assigned to one condition and completed a simulated retail selection task with no awareness of the other condition.

Five response variables were measured: decision time in seconds, purchase behavior (binary), and satisfaction, confidence, and post-choice regret on 1–5 Likert scales. Because the design has only one factor, no interaction term applies.

Key Finding
19.5 sec  vs  10.2 sec

Participants shown 16 options took nearly twice as long to decide as those shown 4 options — a statistically significant difference confirmed by both the Welch t-test (t ≈ 4.80, p < .001) and the one-way ANOVA (F ≈ 23.55, p < .001) at α = 0.05.

No significant differences were found in purchase rate, satisfaction, confidence, or regret. The study supports the cognitive cost of more choices — people needed more time — but did not find evidence of the emotional downsides the Paradox of Choice theory predicts. The data suggest assortment size affects deliberation speed without undermining the purchase experience in this setting.
